Part I: Arrival in the Andes › Revised Draft

The morning air in Cusco carried the sharp, resinous scent of burning eucalyptus leaves and damp adobe. At eleven thousand feet, even climbing the narrow stone steps toward San Blas demanded a deliberate rhythm—two short inhales through the nose, one slow release.

In my rucksack sat two blank pocket notebooks and the lingering habits of graduate workshops where every sentence was weighed for cleverness. Out here, looking across terracotta rooftops toward Ausangate on the horizon, cleverness felt beside the point. The mountains asked for a quieter kind of attention.
